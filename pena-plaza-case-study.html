The Challenge

Launch the public presence now without overbuilding the platform.

Peña Plaza needed a professional web presence for the property, but its long-term tenant-service and payment workflow was still evolving. Rather than delay the public website or force an early software decision, the project focused first on the information prospective tenants and local search engines need today.

The location also needed to be represented accurately. The site was written and structured around Weslaco and the Rio Grande Valley instead of using Renatus's usual San Antonio market language, giving Peña Plaza its own geographic identity and search context.

The goal was a polished commercial-property website that could begin building visibility immediately while remaining easy to extend when leasing or tenant-service needs change.
  • Give Peña Plaza a visual identity and website style distinct from Renatus's other client projects.
  • Make leasing, property, and location information easy to find on desktop and mobile devices.
  • Establish Weslaco and Rio Grande Valley location signals for organic search and answer-engine discovery.
  • Keep the first version lightweight rather than prematurely coupling the site to a payment or tenant-management platform.
  • Preserve a maintainable static architecture with automated deployment through GitHub and Netlify.

The Solution

A focused property website with room to evolve.

Renatus built the site around a clear property story, leasing discovery, local relevance, and a simple deployment workflow.

01

Custom Visual Direction

Developed a warm, understated commercial-property look with Peña Plaza branding, refined neutral tones, and a presentation designed to feel established without copying another Renatus client site.

02

Leasing & Location Structure

Organized the public experience around the information a prospective tenant needs most, including property context, leasing paths, Weslaco location information, and clear ways to continue the conversation.

03

SEO & AEO Foundation

Added search-friendly page structure, metadata, sitemap and robots support, and geographic language aligned with Weslaco and the Rio Grande Valley so the site can build discoverability over time.

04

GitHub-to-Netlify Deployment

Kept the project in a straightforward HTML, CSS, and JavaScript architecture connected to GitHub and Netlify, allowing updates to move from the repository to the live website through the existing deployment workflow.

The marketing site and its GitHub/Netlify deployment workflow can continue independently, so later payment or portal integrations do not have to block the public presence today.
