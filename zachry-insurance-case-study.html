THE PROJECT

I built Zachry Insurance’s website from scratch to help people understand their coverage options, use practical tools, and request guidance. The site is now appearing in Google Search and receiving visits. These are early visibility and usage results; the next step is measuring which visits become qualified inquiries.

Make a complicated service easier to approach

Life insurance brings people to a website with different questions. A new parent may be thinking about protecting their family. Someone reviewing an existing policy may need help understanding what they already have. A prospective agent needs a different kind of information altogether.

Zachry Insurance needed a recognizable brand and a website that could serve those needs clearly. My role through Renatus covered the brand, custom website, educational content, guided coverage tools, inquiry forms, recruiting path, and analytics setup.

The goal was to give visitors a useful starting point and make their next conversation with the business easier.

Connect education, useful tools, and the next step

The design uses a navy-and-gold identity with a resting dalmatian, carried through a responsive website. Behind that visual identity, the build connects four parts of the customer and agent experience:

01 Educational content
Pages on life insurance for parents, term coverage, and other planning questions give visitors context before they contact an agent.
02 Guided coverage tools
The Policy Decoder, Parent Coverage Wizard, AI Coverage Interview, and Final Expense Planner help visitors organize their questions and needs.
03 Structured inquiries
Contact and policy-review forms collect information in a consistent format for follow-up, with a clear confirmation after a successful submission.
04 A separate recruiting path
Career information and agent applications give prospective agents their own route through the site, separate from customer coverage requests.

Search foundations and analytics support those journeys. The business can see where its content appears, how visitors use the site, and whether the inquiry workflow is being measured as intended.

The site is earning search exposure and receiving visits

From June 23 through October 13, 2026, Google Search Console recorded 6,160 impressions and 65 clicks. Its reported click-through rate was 1.06%, with an average position of 47.3.

For the same period, GA4 recorded 312 sessions from 121 active users on the production website, with 677 page views. Of those sessions, 128 were attributed to Organic Search: 127 to Google and one to Bing.

GA4 also recorded 166 engaged sessions and a 53.21% engagement rate. These describe recorded site activity; they are not counts of prospective customers. The 128 Organic Search sessions are part of the 312 total sessions, and they should not be added to the 65 Search Console clicks.

Make the inquiry tracking worth relying on

Measurement was part of the work, too. On October 15, the live implementation was updated to centralize tracking for successful insurance inquiries, reduce duplicate event sends, and exclude localhost, preview sites, and browsers that opted out.

An About-page event was removed from GA4 key events. The successful-inquiry event, generate_lead, was already marked as a key event when the live settings were inspected and was retained.

CONTROLLED VERIFICATION / OCTOBER 15

A labeled contact-form test produced one generate_lead event and one key event in GA4 Realtime, with form_name=contact-request. This verified that test submission’s tracking. It is outside the reporting window and is not counted as a customer inquiry.

The historical report contained three generate_lead events with unverified business outcomes and ten About-page key events. Those records do not establish a reliable website lead conversion rate. The corrected setup gives future reporting a clearer basis for separating successful form submissions from ordinary browsing.

Use the evidence to choose the next improvements

The project now has a working website, educational content, guided tools, and a verified inquiry-tracking path. My priorities for the next stage are to:

  • Review pages with exposure but few clicks. Start with the parents article and check how well the page answers the searches it appears for.
  • Make the next step clear. Connect relevant educational pages to a useful tool or inquiry path.
  • Separate customer and recruiting outcomes. Measure coverage inquiries and agent applications according to their different business goals.
  • Connect inquiries to follow-up. Verify which submissions are genuine prospects and whether they progress to a quote or policy before reporting business results.

For Renatus, this is the value of continuing beyond the initial build: the website gives the business something useful to operate, and the evidence helps decide what deserves attention next.
