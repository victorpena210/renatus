Websites for insurance agencies

Help prospects understand your services and reach the right person without turning your public website into a sensitive-data collection system.

Review your inquiry journeyDiscuss your project

A useful agency website should make the next conversation easier. Renatus starts with the lines of business you actually offer, the people you serve, and the information an agent needs to route an inquiry. The goal is a clear service website and a dependable handoff—not a generic template filled with promises your team has not approved.

01 / The workflow

Organize the site around real customer questions

Separate important coverage categories into useful pages, explain your service area accurately, and make phone and inquiry options easy to find. Your team approves coverage descriptions and any insurer-related statements before publication. A clear first screen and specific service content can be evaluated without claiming that a redesign guarantees more policy sales.

02 / The workflow

Keep the first inquiry simple

Collect only the contact and routing information needed to start the conversation. Sensitive policy, payment, identity, or health information belongs in an appropriately designed secure workflow, not an ordinary marketing form. Existing carrier or agency tools can remain the system of record while the website directs visitors to the correct next step.

03 / The workflow

Define what happens after someone submits

Map who receives a request, how the visitor sees confirmation, and how your staff records follow-up. Test the complete path on a phone as well as a desktop. Where an integration is needed, confirm access, vendor support, data fields, failure handling, and recurring charges before committing to a connection.

A sensible first scope

A first release might include an agency homepage, priority service pages, an approved About page, clear contact details, and a tested inquiry form. Quoting integrations and customer portals are separate scope decisions.

Every proposal should identify deliverables, exclusions, third-party costs, acceptance tests, and ongoing responsibilities. Review Renatus pricing and engagement options.
