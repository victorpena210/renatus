Client portals & authenticated workflows

Give customers the information they should have—and no more

Plan secure access, useful status information, and clear customer actions before building a portal.

Check whether a portal solves your problemDiscuss your project

A portal should remove a defined obstacle, such as repeated status calls, scattered approvals, or a confusing document handoff. Renatus begins by identifying what the customer needs to see and what your staff needs to manage. If an existing product provides a suitable portal, using it may be more sensible than maintaining another application.

01 / The workflow

Start with one useful customer journey

Define the status, action, or exchange the portal will support. Make the distinction between view-only information and actions that change business records. Decide how customers receive access and what happens when an invitation expires, an email changes, or an account is no longer authorized.

02 / The workflow

Design permissions at the data boundary

Do not rely on hiding a button to protect a record. Specify server-side authorization, organization boundaries where needed, role-specific access, and tests for attempts to reach another customer’s data. Recovery flows and administrator access should be reviewed alongside normal sign-in. No design can be honestly described as hack-proof.

03 / The workflow

Plan operations and ownership

Discuss backups, monitoring, incident response, data export, retention, and who maintains the system. Payment processing and sensitive document exchange introduce additional requirements that must be assessed for the specific use case. A portal mockup or successful login is not evidence that these operational controls are complete.

A sensible first scope

A first scope might be authenticated status visibility with one approved action. Document storage, payments, messaging, integrations, and multiple organizations are separate requirements, not assumed extras.

Every proposal should identify deliverables, exclusions, third-party costs, acceptance tests, and ongoing responsibilities. Review Renatus pricing and engagement options.
