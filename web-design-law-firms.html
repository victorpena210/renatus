Law firms

Clear websites and dependable intake for law firms

Explain your practice and route inquiries into a reviewed intake process, without treating a public form as a secure client portal.

Check the path from visitor to inquiryDiscuss your project

Renatus approaches a law-firm website as a service-information and intake project. The first questions are which matters the firm handles, who reviews inquiries, and what the visitor should understand before sharing information. The firm reviews all professional statements, disclaimers, and intake wording; Renatus builds the agreed technical workflow.

01 / The workflow

Make the practice areas understandable

Create useful pages that explain the services the firm chooses to describe publicly, its genuine service area, and the next step for contacting the team. Use approved attorney information and genuine work-related evidence where appropriate. Avoid automated pages that simply replace one location name with another or imply a guaranteed case outcome.

02 / The workflow

Design intake around an actual review process

A first-contact form can capture basic routing information while telling visitors not to submit confidential records through that channel. Confirm who monitors submissions and what the acknowledgment should say. Conflict review, representation decisions, and professional obligations stay with the firm’s established process; the website must not imply those decisions happen automatically.

03 / The workflow

Separate marketing access from client access

Public pages, appointment requests, and authenticated document exchange have different requirements. A proposed portal needs its own discussion of permissions, retention, access recovery, logging, and integrations. Existing practice-management software may already provide the right workflow, so the first task is to evaluate it rather than replace it by default.

A sensible first scope

A practical first scope is approved service content, mobile-friendly navigation, a tested contact route, and inquiry measurement. Secure document exchange and practice-management integrations are assessed separately.
