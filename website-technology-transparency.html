Before You Sign

A clear proposal should identify the technology behind the website.

  • Website platform and hosting
  • Themes, builders, plugins, and licenses
  • Domain, account, content, and file ownership

Review the Questions

Technology Should Not Be a Mystery

Software choices affect performance, recurring costs, security, editing, portability, and maintenance. A provider should be able to describe those choices without hiding behind technical jargon.

Why Some Web Companies Avoid Explaining the Software

There is no single explanation. Sometimes the reason is harmless. Other times, the lack of a clear answer reveals a problem worth investigating before you sign a contract.

They Want to Keep the Sale Simple

Some agencies believe platform details will overwhelm the client, so they lead with design and marketing outcomes instead of the technology used to produce them.

They Sell Outcomes First

A full-service agency may see the website as one part of a larger campaign involving branding, content, advertising, social media, and lead generation.

The Sales and Technical Teams Are Separate

The person selling the project may not build websites and may not know which theme, page builder, plugins, hosting, or integrations the production team plans to use.

The Stack Includes Recurring Licenses

Themes, page builders, SEO tools, form plugins, security products, and other services may renew annually. Those costs should be identified before development begins.

They Use a Standardized System

An agency may use the same platform for most clients because it supports its internal workflow—even when a smaller or simpler website could meet the client's needs.

Portability Is Complicated

Proprietary tools, agency-owned licenses, hosted builders, and connected services can make moving a website more complicated. The exit process should be explained in advance.

The real warning sign: An agency does not have to lead every sales conversation with software details. But if you ask a direct question about the platform, licenses, ownership, or ongoing costs, you deserve a direct and understandable answer.

10 Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Web Designer

The answers will help you understand what you are buying, what you will continue paying for, and how much control your business retains.

  1. What platform, framework, or content-management system will my website use?
  2. Will the design use a premade theme, page builder, template, or custom code?
  3. Which plugins, integrations, or outside services will the website depend on?
  4. Which licenses renew, how much do they cost, and who is responsible for them?
  5. Who will be listed as the domain registrant, and what does the contract say about ownership of the content, design, website files, and connected accounts? [1]
  6. Will my business be an owner of its Search Console property and receive appropriate access to analytics and other search accounts? [2]
  7. Where will the website be hosted, and can its files, content, and data be moved to another provider? [3]
  8. Who handles software updates, backups, security monitoring, recovery, and compatibility problems? [4] [5]
  9. How will speed, mobile usability, accessibility, SEO, and forms be tested?
  10. What remains available to my business if I cancel the maintenance agreement?

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers about website technology, ownership, and recurring costs.

Should a web designer tell me what platform my website uses?

Yes. A provider should be able to identify the platform, hosting, theme or page builder, important third-party services, and known recurring software costs in plain English.

Who should own my business website?

The contract should clearly explain who owns the domain, content, design, website files, accounts, and licensed software. It should also explain what remains available if the service relationship ends.

Are WordPress plugins included in the website price?

It depends on the provider. Some plugins are free, while others require recurring licenses. Ask who owns each license, who renews it, and what happens if it expires.

Can I move my website to another hosting company?

Portability depends on the platform, contract, licenses, and connected services. Ask what files and data can be transferred and whether migration assistance is included.

What does Renatus explain before development?

Renatus explains the recommended platform, hosting approach, important third-party services, known recurring costs, update process, and the responsibilities of both Renatus and the client.

Does transparency require understanding all the code?

No. The provider's job is to translate the important technical decisions into understandable business terms so you can make an informed choice without becoming a developer.
