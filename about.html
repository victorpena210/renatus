A Lesson That Shaped Renatus

The client should not have to pay twice for a technology decision they did not make.

Before Renatus, I worked at an advertising agency where I helped maintain websites built with WordPress and Joomla. I saw firsthand how a technology choice made by the provider could become the client's problem years later. Some sites had been built on Joomla even when other widely supported content-management options were available. When those sites became difficult to maintain or stopped working as expected, the proposed answer could be another paid website project.

What stayed with me was that the client usually had not chosen the CMS or understood the long-term consequences of that decision. The technology had been selected for them, but they were still expected to absorb the cost when that choice became a problem. That experience changed the way I think about web development.

I saw a similar pattern with search visibility. From what I personally observed, SEO was often limited to the defaults supplied by the CMS or theme and whatever an SEO plugin provided once it was enabled. I did not see much emphasis on building a broader search strategy around the client's actual services, experience, customer questions, or local market.

That taught me an important distinction: installing an SEO plugin is useful, but it is not an SEO strategy by itself. Search visibility also depends on page structure, useful first-hand content, internal linking, technical performance, structured data, indexing, and measuring what people actually find through search. That lesson now informs Renatus's SEO and AEO approach.

At Renatus, a rebuild is not automatically the first recommendation. I want to understand what actually failed, whether the existing website can reasonably be repaired, what the business needs next, and which approach leaves the client in a better position several years from now. The platform, recurring costs, ownership, maintenance expectations, and exit options should be understandable before the client commits.

The principle: build for the client's long-term interests, not for unnecessary dependence on the person who built the website.
