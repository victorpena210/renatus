Contractors & home-service businesses

Websites that make the next estimate easier

Show real work, explain the jobs you take, and give prospective customers a clear route to request an estimate.

Review your estimate-request pathDiscuss your project

An estimate request is more useful when the customer understands what you do and your team receives enough information to follow up. Renatus builds contractor websites around actual services, a genuine operating area, and a manageable inquiry process. The website should support the way estimates are qualified and scheduled—not promise instant prices for work that needs an inspection.

01 / The workflow

Use the work you can actually show

Organize approved project photos, service explanations, and genuine customer feedback around the jobs you want to discuss. Describe project context without inventing savings, completion times, or performance guarantees. Confirm permission to publish customer images, addresses, or identifying details before they appear in a portfolio.

02 / The workflow

Ask enough to route the inquiry

Define the service category, approximate location, contact method, and useful project notes. Keep the form short enough to complete on a phone. A later, appropriately designed upload step may be better than asking for extensive documents or photos immediately. Tell visitors whether they are requesting a callback, an appointment, or a formal estimate.

03 / The workflow

Make follow-up visible to your team

Decide where the request goes, who owns it, and how its status changes. An existing scheduling or customer-management tool may be enough; a small integration can be considered when information otherwise gets copied repeatedly. Measure completed requests and actual follow-up rather than counting every button click as a booked job.

A sensible first scope

Begin with priority service pages, approved examples of work, genuine service-area information, a tested estimate request, and clear ownership of incoming inquiries. Automated quoting requires a separate model and validation.

Every proposal should identify deliverables, exclusions, third-party costs, acceptance tests, and ongoing responsibilities. Review Renatus pricing and engagement options.
